# Research and Clinical Review

## Source selection

Use at least three independent procedural videos for each distinct sequence. Prefer, in order:

1. University or teaching-hospital demonstrations.
2. Professional society or government clinical education.
3. Clinician-led specialty education with visible full-body technique.

Supplement the videos with at least one guideline, peer-reviewed article, or institutional procedure. Do not count reposts of the same recording as independent sources. Avoid clips that omit the start, transition, or return position.

## Required source record

For each video record `title`, `publisher`, `url`, `accessed_on`, the maneuver/side/variant shown, one timestamp per key movement, observations, and a quality of `primary`, `supporting`, or `excluded`. Add an exclusion reason when excluded.

Use direct video URLs. A timestamp must identify the visible action, not merely a chapter heading copied without verification.

## Consensus table

Create a row for every key movement and compare all sources. Record anatomical side and screen-side convention, body movement and axis, head movement and approximate angle, fixed body parts and support points, speed, hold, source agreement, and accepted variation.

Add a directional landmark check whenever the camera faces the patient. Record where the nose, chin, sternum, and feet should point on screen. Clinical review must inspect the artwork itself; labels such as `right 45 degrees` do not prove that the illustrated rotation is correct.

When sources disagree, prefer the authoritative written source and explain the choice. Keep naming explicit because terms such as Gufoni, modified Gufoni, and Appiani are not used consistently across publications.

## Review issues

Each issue in `review.json` needs an ID, frame, category, finding, source URLs, status, resolution, and correction cycle. Allowed statuses are `open`, `fixed`, `accepted-variant`, and `pass`. An accepted variant needs a written source and domain-owner approval.

## Clinical sign-off

Record reviewer name or role, date, reviewed maneuver/side/variant, and status. The skill can prepare evidence and identify inconsistencies; it must not impersonate clinical sign-off.
